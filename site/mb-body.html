01 Can the brain itself hold the phrase?

Ready for a new experiment

Every earlier lab learned in an external readout. Here nothing outside the brain learns. Letters arrive as sparse olfactory-style codes on projection neurons, the only synapses that change are the existing Kenyon-cell-to-MBON connections of the mushroom body, and the readout is a fixed vote among MBON groups. During recall the winning letter is fed straight back as the next cue.

In-brain learning, with an imposed model and an external teacher. The slower time constants, the rescaled weights, the sparse letter codes and the supervised three-factor rule are the experimenter’s choices. They are not measured fruit-fly physiology, and the teacher that says which MBON group should have won is not a fly’s dopamine system.

03 Protocol & scientific limits+

Cells. Kenyon cells, mushroom-body output neurons (MBONs) and uniglomerular antennal-lobe projection neurons are taken from the public FlyWire v783 classification table and mapped onto the browser model’s neuron indices; the annotation file records the source hashes. The eight input codes are a fixed seeded partition of the 277 projection neurons; the eight output groups are a fixed seeded partition of the 96 MBONs. No cell was hand-picked.

Task and timing. The reference is “to be or not to be”. Each cue drives its code at 100 Hz for 100 ms, then a 25 ms gap. MBON spikes are counted over the whole 125 ms; the group with the most spikes is the decision, with ties broken by the groups’ mean quantized membrane voltage and then by group order. In the plastic and frozen conditions one network runs through a whole episode without reset; the reset condition replaces all neural state at every cue onset.

What learns. During eight teacher-forced training episodes, after each decision in which the target group was not the strict winner, every Kenyon cell that spiked in the window strengthens its synapses onto the target group and weakens its synapses onto the strongest rival group, in proportion to its spike count, within fixed bounds. Only existing Kenyon-cell-to-MBON synapses change. Learning is then switched off for three teacher-forced diagnostic episodes and six autonomous recall episodes, in which the decoded letter becomes the next cue, END stops the episode, and a fixed cap does not depend on the phrase length.

The imposed model. Time constants are multiplied by 10 and all weights by 0.3. That scale was chosen because the original weights make two thirds of all Kenyon cells fire for every letter, identically, while a scale of 0.1 leaves the mushroom body silent; at 0.3 a letter activates a few percent of Kenyon cells with codes that differ by letter and by position in the phrase. This is a calibration, not a measurement of fly physiology.

Interpretation. The frozen condition shows what the fixed readout does without learning. The reset condition shows what the synapses can learn from the current letter alone; it cannot beat the no-memory comparator except by chance. A plastic chain beyond that ceiling means position information carried in retained neural state was written into synapses the network already has. It does not show how flies learn, does not involve dopamine neurons, and is not memory for a text.

Reproducibility. Export the complete protocol, both neural parameter sets, the annotation counts and plastic slot list, initial and learned synaptic weights per condition, every decision’s group counts and Kenyon-cell activity, cues, seeds and graph hashes. All errors are retained. New loads default to 1×; actual simulation speed depends on the device. The first run downloads about 49 MB and then prepares the rescaled network.
