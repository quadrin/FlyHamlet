About this experiment +

The FlyWire connectome links 139,255 neurons in a simulated fruit-fly brain. Each recording follows 300 seconds in a 90 × 30 mm arena. Letters are assigned to the 3 × 9 grid by a seeded permutation.

The fly walks at a base speed of 8 mm/s. Nearby walls send proximity-scaled Poisson input to the eye’s LC4 + LPLC2 looming detectors; the network drives turning neurons on the opposite side, steering the fly away. Turning rates use a 50 ms window. The giant-fiber escape signal is recorded but does not control walking.

Randomness comes from the injected Poisson inputs. The brain’s wiring shapes that noise. The hovering fly is a visual interpretation of the recorded walking trajectory, with enlarged artwork for visibility. This page replays recordings; it does not run the simulation.
